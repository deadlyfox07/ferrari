Chapter 01 A LEGACY FORGED ON THE TRACK

PRANCING HORSE HERITAGE

Ferrari has spent over seven decades defining the upper limit of automotive performance. Born from the racing circuits of Maranello, every road car Ferrari produces carries the DNA of its Formula 1 programme. The LaFerrari is the ultimate expression of this philosophy — a halo car that concentrates every technological advancement into a single, breathtaking machine.

"LaFerrari is not merely the best Ferrari ever made. It is the culmination of everything Ferrari knows about performance, aerodynamics, and the marriage of man and machine."

Limited to just 499 road-going examples — plus a solitary auction unit for charity — each LaFerrari was hand-assembled in Maranello, with each buyer personally approved by Ferrari's chairman. Ownership is not purchased; it is earned.

1947

Ferrari Founded

Enzo Ferrari establishes Scuderia Ferrari in Maranello, Italy — laying the foundations for the greatest automotive dynasty in history.

1987

Ferrari F40

The raw, twin-turbocharged icon becomes the first production car to break 200mph, marking a new era of hypercar performance.

1995

Ferrari F50

Inspired directly by Formula 1 technology, the F50's naturally-aspirated V12 revved to 8,500rpm — a pure road-going racing car.

2002

Ferrari Enzo

Named after the founder himself, the Enzo pushed aerodynamic and mechanical boundaries using then-current F1 technology transfer.

2013

Ferrari LaFerrari

The successor to the Enzo arrives. With 963CV of combined hybrid power, it becomes the most powerful and advanced production Ferrari ever made.

Chapter 02 DIRECT FROM FORMULA 1

THE HY-KERS SYSTEM

The LaFerrari's revolutionary HY-KERS (Hybrid Kinetic Energy Recovery System) did not originate in a road car laboratory. It was conceived on the Formula 1 grid — where every joule of energy matters. Ferrari's engineers ported this technology directly to the road, creating a system that works in seamless harmony with the V12 combustion engine.

800 CV — V12 Engine
163 CV — Electric Motor
963 CV — Total Output
6.3L V12 Displacement
9,250 RPM Redline
900 Nm Total Torque

Unlike conventional hybrid systems that prioritise fuel economy, the HY-KERS is engineered purely for performance. The electric motor fills in torque where the V12 naturally has gaps, creating a completely seamless and linear power delivery across the entire rev range.

Energy is recovered under braking and deceleration, storing kinetic energy in a high-density lithium-ion battery pack positioned low behind the seats to maintain an optimal centre of gravity.

Chapter 03 FORM FOLLOWS FUNCTION — BEAUTIFULLY

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The LaFerrari's body was not sculpted by designers with clay — it was shaped by aerodynamicists in the wind tunnel. Every crease, vent, and surface has a measurable aerodynamic purpose. Yet the result is one of the most visually striking automobiles ever created, proving that ultimate performance and breathtaking beauty are not mutually exclusive.

Aerodynamics

Active Downforce

The active rear wing and underbody flaps generate 410kg of downforce at 200 km/h — without adding a single gram of drag. This is active aerodynamics in its purest form.

Materials

Carbon Everywhere

The entire monocoque chassis, bodywork, and structural components are formed from aerospace-grade carbon fibre, keeping the dry weight to just 1,255kg — remarkable for a car of this power.

Cockpit

Driver Focused

The pedal box and steering column adjust to the driver — not the other way around. The cockpit is designed around the human body, putting every control within instinctive reach at 350 km/h.

Drag coefficient of just 0.299 Cd — extraordinary for a car generating 410kg of aerodynamic downforce. The laws of physics, bent to Ferrari's will.

Chapter 04 AN ETERNAL MARK ON AUTOMOTIVE HISTORY

THE LEGACY

The LaFerrari changed what the world believed was possible from a road car. When it debuted at the 2013 Geneva Motor Show, it did not merely compete with the McLaren P1 and Porsche 918 Spyder — it redefined the benchmark entirely. It was the first Ferrari to use a full hybrid drivetrain, and its performance figures were unprecedented.

In 2016, the LaFerrari Aperta — an open-top version — was revealed at the Paris Motor Show, limited to just 210 units. A single Aperta was also auctioned for a staggering $7 million, making it the most expensive new car ever sold at auction.
